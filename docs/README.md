# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

Welcome to OctoAcme's project management documentation hub. This README is the entry point for new and existing contributors to understand how work moves from idea to delivery and continuous improvement.

## Process Overview

OctoAcme follows a lightweight, structured lifecycle to keep teams aligned and outcomes measurable:
- **Initiation:** Define the problem, desired outcomes, success metrics, key stakeholders, and initial risks before approving work.
- **Planning:** Break work into shippable increments, prioritize and estimate backlog items, define acceptance criteria and Definition of Done, and map dependencies and milestones.
- **Execution & Tracking:** Deliver iteratively through project board workflows, regular team rituals, and PR-based collaboration with clear progress and blocker visibility.
- **Release & Deployment:** Ship through staged, checklist-driven releases with verification, stakeholder communication, and rollback readiness.
- **Retrospective & Continuous Improvement:** Capture lessons after sprints, releases, milestones, and incidents, then convert them into owned action items.

## Roles and Personas

OctoAcme uses clear, cross-functional role ownership:
- **Project Managers** coordinate delivery plans, schedules, risk management, and stakeholder communication.
- **Product Managers** define outcomes, prioritize roadmap/backlog work, and measure business and customer impact.
- **Developers** design, implement, test, and review software while identifying technical risks and proposing mitigations.
- **QA/Testing contributors** validate acceptance criteria and quality standards through automated and manual testing.
- **Stakeholders/Sponsors** provide strategic input, approvals, and escalation support when needed.

## Communication, Tracking, and Risk Escalation

Teams maintain a single source of truth (project docs/boards), use recurring status rhythms (standups, weekly syncs, milestone updates), and report progress with clear next steps and asks. Risks are tracked in a risk register and reviewed regularly, with escalation paths moving from team triage to PM, then Product Lead, and finally sponsor-level escalation for business-critical blockers. Security-sensitive incidents follow dedicated security escalation processes.

## Quality Assurance and Release Practices

Quality is built into delivery, not deferred to the end. OctoAcme emphasizes unit tests for new logic, integration and smoke testing for critical flows, CI checks (including linting and security scanning), and manual QA when needed for acceptance. Release readiness requires completed acceptance criteria, passing CI/security checks, release notes, and a rollback/mitigation plan, followed by post-deploy verification and stakeholder communication.
